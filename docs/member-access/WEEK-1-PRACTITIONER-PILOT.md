# Week 1 — from accumulated knowledge to one useful move

Status: founder-review draft, practitioner pilot variant. Does not overwrite the original 30-day pillar map. Not published to the lesson database or served as paid lessons. Themes derive from Major's 19–20 October interview; anecdotes remain founder-attributed. Use anonymous fictional examples and copied sample files. Complete 30-day curriculum remains in development.

## Day 1 — Start where you are

Learn: A useful starting point is a person and a problem, not a list of tools. Major describes calls and downloaded files as ordinary places where unfinished work accumulated.

Example: A practitioner has useful teaching recordings but cannot find an answer when a client asks a familiar question.

Major Move: Complete “I want to help ___ with ___. A small useful result would look like ___.” Keep the first result within your control: a clear resource index rather than guaranteed sales.

Create: A one-page starting-point note. Record who benefits, the problem and one small result.

Reflect: What do you already know? What needs checking?

Completion: Explain the problem and a realistic first result in your own words. No public sharing required.

## Day 2 — Notice where energy leaks

Learn: Repeating a task is a clue about where a digital worker could help. Notice friction before buying or connecting tools. Major describes becoming tired after calls and losing momentum on follow-up.

Major Move: List three repeated tasks. For each, record the input, the desired output, what causes delay and the current human decision. Choose one to improve.

Create: A three-row friction map. Optional estimates of time are guesses until observed; do not invent savings.

Reflect: Which task is frequent enough to matter and small enough to try safely?

Completion: Select one task with a clearly described input and output.

## Day 3 — Find knowledge you already have

Learn: The first resource may already exist in a recording, worksheet or note. You do not need to organize an entire archive to begin.

Major Move: Choose five resources you own or have permission to use. Record their title, location, intended audience, topic, version and permission status. Use placeholders for private client material; do not upload it to a public tool.

Create: A small resource inventory. Identify one recurring question those resources could help answer.

Reflect: Which resource is useful today, and which needs updating or rights review?

Completion: Find the chosen resource again using your inventory.

## Day 4 — From conversation to clear action

Learn: Major describes capturing conversations and turning them into consistent follow-up briefs. A transcript can contain recognition errors; statements are not automatically agreements.

Major Move: Use a fictional conversation supplied by a facilitator: “I have five recordings. I cannot find the right one. Could we create an index? I can send titles tomorrow. We have not decided whether to publish anything.” Draft a brief with the problem, proposed action, actual commitments, open questions and next steps. Correct any invented agreement.

Create: A reviewed one-page brief. A digital worker's instruction includes a role, output structure, example, boundaries and review rule.

Reflect: What did the draft assume that the speaker did not say?

Completion: Separate the proposed index from the unresolved publication decision. For real recording/transcription, obtain appropriate permission before capture and use an approved tool.

## Day 5 — One place in, everything findable

Learn: A simple intake process can reduce searching. Start with a small set, not an automated purge of the Downloads folder.

Major Move: Copy ten non-sensitive sample files into a practice folder. Make an index using three consistent tags: project, topic and stage. Add meaningful filenames and source/version links. Mark suspected duplicates for review; preserve originals and do not auto-delete.

Create: An intake rule and file index. Search by a natural phrase and by tags; note what is findable and what is missing.

Reflect: Did two different names hide the same resource? What evidence would establish a true duplicate?

Completion: Locate a chosen file through the index without scrolling through every file. Do not promise faster inference or a faster computer from tidying alone.

## Day 6 — Define your words, preserve your approach

Learn: Familiar professional terms may mean different things to different people. A shared glossary can support understanding while preserving a practitioner's own language.

Major Move: Choose three terms from your work. For each, write your meaning, a plain-language explanation and one practical example. Ask a facilitator or use a self-review: could someone explain the action without repeating the term?

Create: A mini glossary plus a short written explanation. Optional audio/script or visual example should teach the same action. A generated discussion is reviewed and labeled, not presented as independent expert testimony.

Reflect: Which word sounds clear to you but could confuse your intended person?

Completion: Rewrite one confusing explanation with a concrete example and a clear next step.

## Day 7 — Shape one useful resource

Learn: A first offer connects a defined person, a problem, an action and a reasonable first result. Payment or downloads alone do not show implementation.

Major Move: Use one resource from Day 3 to answer the recurring question. Draft a short guide with one action, one worked example, a likely barrier and a way to check progress. Write what support would be included and where additional consulting begins. This is a proposal, not a requirement to sell during Week 1.

Create: A one-page useful resource and an offer card: person, problem, first result, action, barrier, support and evidence. Include a private weekly reflection on effort and sustainable pace.

Reflect: Could someone use the guide without you explaining every sentence? What would you ask them after trying it?

Completion: Explain what the user will do and how you will learn whether it helped. Optional feedback must use permission-cleared material. Keep claims proportional to evidence.

## Delivery and review

Every lesson needs a final founder/facilitator review, accessible transcript if audio is added, and age adaptation before a youth edition. No tools, recordings, worksheets or synthetic audio are represented as completed media here. Adult practitioner pilot first; the wider Academy remains for multiple audiences.

Track creation effort separately from time per client. Week 1 success is one organized resource set, a practical action and a draft useful resource—not a guaranteed conversion or income claim. Paid live implementation groups, the included 45-minute private session and the separate public knowledge/build funnels retain their established boundaries.
