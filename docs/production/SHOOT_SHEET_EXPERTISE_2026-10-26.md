## The one idea

Undocumented knowledge is risk, not mastery. It can't be **improved** (nobody can see
it to question it), **delegated** (nothing to hand over), **sold** (a buyer is buying
you) or **inherited**.

## Chunks (filming targets, not timestamps)

1. **Open on your line, 0–1 min.** *"When you want to automate something, you have to
   itemise everything that you do."* Then: that sentence is the whole video.
2. **Knowing how vs having a process, 1–3 min.** Both are real; the gap between them is
   where businesses quietly stall. The four "can'ts" above.
3. **Step four, 3–5 min.** You write seven steps and step four turns out to be *"and
   then I look at it and I know."* That's the step that matters, and the one you never
   examined. Improvisation wearing the costume of expertise.
4. **Why people skip it, 5–6 min.** Writing it down is slower, once. Then faster
   forever. The quieter reason: you might find out it isn't that complicated.
   Replaceable in your *tasks*, irreplaceable in your *judgment*.
   Your second line: *"Most of us do things effortlessly and don't realise that not
   everybody else can do these things."* Effortless is valuable, and invisible to you
   *because* it's effortless.
5. **The demo, 6–9 min.** Do the System Audit on paper, on camera, with the real task
   you picked. Number the steps, then go back and circle every improvisation. Say out
   loud for each one: is it a rule I haven't written yet, or judgment that stays with
   me? Use your real task and your real answers. Don't invent a client or a result.
6. **Assignment and CTA, 9–10 min.** Then the outro.

## The assignment: System Audit (manuscript rubric)

Pick **one** repeated task: not the biggest, one you did this week and will do next
week. Write it as numbered steps for someone capable who has never seen it. Mark every
place you improvised: a judgment call, a check that wasn't on the list, "it felt off."

| Step | Input | Output | Improvised? | Handoff owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What happens | What you need first | What exists after | Judgment, not a rule | A person, an agent, or *me, for now* |

Say it plainly: **the improvisation points aren't failures. They're the system's
frontier.** Deciding which are unwritten rules and which are real judgment is the work.
If they did the Dependency Score from the Bottleneck video, they can take one of their
3s and audit that.
